Human Biomechanics & Intelligent Systems

Understand humans. Build intelligent systems.

My research integrates human biomechanics, wearable sensing, computational modeling, and artificial intelligence to understand human responses to mechanical environments and to develop personalized, adaptive human–machine systems.

I lead the Human Biomechanics & Intelligent Systems Laboratory (HBIS Lab) at the University of New Brunswick. Our work spans occupational and transportation biomechanics, human digital twins, physics-guided AI, intelligent protection, assistive systems, and adaptive rehabilitation.

Selected Research Themes

  • Human vibration and intelligent protection: biodynamic response, vibration transmission, protective interfaces, and adaptive isolation.
  • Personalized human digital twins: integration of wearable data, system identification, and biomechanical models for individualized prediction.
  • Physics-guided AI for biomechanics: hybrid mechanistic/data-driven models that preserve physical interpretability while learning from experimental data.
  • Adaptive human–machine interaction: intelligent seating, assistive technologies, rehabilitation systems, and other human-in-the-loop devices.
